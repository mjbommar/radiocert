Power Supplies and Voltage Regulators

Tell series from shunt and linear from switching regulators, compute a pass transistor's dissipation, and say what equalising resistors and step-start do.

9:08
transcript
11 min readE7DComponents & CircuitsadvancedDraft

Two ways to regulate

How does a linear electronic voltage regulator work?

The conduction of a control element is varied to maintain a constant output voltage.

How does a switchmode voltage regulator work?

By varying the duty cycle of pulses input to a filter.

A linear regulator burns off the difference in a pass element — quiet, simple, and inefficient. A switching regulator chops the input and filters the result, adjusting the duty cycle to set the output — efficient, small, and noisy.

Why is a switching type power supply less expensive and lighter than an equivalent linear power supply?

The high frequency inverter design uses much smaller transformers and filter components for an equivalent power output

The same argument as the General lesson: magnetics shrink with frequency.

Series versus shunt

Which of the following describes a three-terminal voltage regulator?

A series regulator.

Which of the following types of linear voltage regulator operates by loading the unregulated voltage source?

A shunt regulator

A series regulator puts the control element in series with the load and varies its resistance — the familiar 78xx three-terminal part.

A shunt regulator sits across the load and draws whatever current the load does not, keeping the total constant. Simpler, but it dissipates most when the load draws least, so it is only sensible at low power. A Zener used as a regulator is a shunt regulator.

What device is used as a stable voltage reference?

A Zener diode.

Its reverse breakdown voltage is sharply defined and stable, which is exactly what a reference needs.

Reading figure E7-2

What type of circuit is shown in Figure E7-2?

Linear voltage regulator.

What is the purpose of Q1 in the circuit shown in Figure E7-2?

It controls the current to keep the output voltage constant

Q1 is the pass transistor — the control element in series with the load, exactly as the definition describes.

What is the purpose of C2 in the circuit shown in Figure E7-2?

It bypasses rectifier output ripple around D1

D1 is the Zener reference, and any ripple appearing across it would be amplified straight through to the output. C2 shunts that ripple to ground, keeping the reference clean. This is why a good linear regulator has a capacitor across its reference and a poor one does not.

Dissipation and dropout

Which of the following calculates power dissipated by a series linear voltage regulator?

Voltage difference from input to output multiplied by output current.

P=(Vin−Vout)×IoutP = (V_{in} - V_{out}) \times I_{out}P=(Vin​−Vout​)×Iout​

The pass element carries the full load current with the full voltage difference across it, and P = E × I. A 5 V regulator fed from 15 V at 1 A dissipates (15−5) × 1 = 10 watts — twice what it delivers.

That single calculation explains every oversized heat sink you have ever seen, and why switching regulators displaced linear ones for anything drawing real current.

What is the dropout voltage of a linear voltage regulator?

Minimum input-to-output voltage required to maintain regulation.

Fall below it and the regulator stops regulating and simply follows the input. A standard 78xx needs about 2 V; a low-dropout part manages a few hundred millivolts, which matters when running a 12 V regulator from a battery that sags to 12.5.

High-voltage supply refinements

Two features specific to the supplies inside tube amplifiers.

What is the purpose of connecting equal-value resistors across power supply filter capacitors connected in series?

All these choices are correct — to equalise the voltage across each capacitor, to discharge the capacitors when the supply is off, and to provide a minimum load.

Capacitors in series divide voltage according to their leakage, not their capacitance — so without equalising resistors one may take far more than its rated share and fail, cascading into the others. The same resistors double as bleeders and as a minimum load.

What is the purpose of a step-start circuit in a high-voltage power supply?

To allow the filter capacitors to charge gradually.

At switch-on, uncharged filter capacitors look like a short circuit. A step-start inserts a resistor for a moment, then shorts it out once the capacitors have partly charged — sparing the rectifiers, the transformer, and the mains breaker from a very large inrush.

Batteries and inverters

How is battery operating time calculated?

Capacity in amp-hours divided by average current.

The General figure, restated. Average, not peak.

What is the purpose of an inverter connected to a solar panel output?

Convert the panel’s output from DC to AC.

An inverter makes AC from DC. It is not a charge controller and not a regulator — three different devices for three different jobs, and the solar questions in the General track ask about the other two.

Check yourself

  1. A linear regulator delivers 12 V at 3 A from an 18 V input. Dissipation?
  2. Why do series filter capacitors in a high-voltage supply need equalising resistors?
  3. Your 12 V regulator loses regulation when the battery sags to 12.8 V. What specification have you hit?
Answers
  1. (18 − 12) × 3 = 18 watts in the pass element — half again what it delivers.
  2. Series capacitors divide voltage by leakage, not capacitance, so one can exceed its rating and fail. The resistors equalise the division, bleed the charge, and provide a minimum load.
  3. Dropout voltage — the minimum input-to-output difference needed to regulate. A low-dropout regulator would still be working.
