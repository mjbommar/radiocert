Contesting, DX, Remote Operation, and Logging

Work a DX pile-up efficiently, submit a contest log in the right format, confirm contacts electronically, and understand what latency does to remote operating.

Working a pile-up

How should you generally identify your station when attempting to contact a DX station during a contest or in a pileup?

Send your full call sign once or twice

Full call sign — partial calls waste everyone’s time when the DX comes back to a fragment and three stations answer. Once or twice — longer and you are still transmitting when they reply.

Why do DX stations often transmit and receive on different frequencies?

All these choices are correct — to separate the DX station from the pile-up, to improve operating efficiency, and to allow more stations to be worked.

Split operation. With everyone calling on one frequency, nobody hears the DX and the DX hears mush. Split puts the DX in the clear and spreads callers across a few kilohertz, so the DX can pick individual stations out. It is the dual-VFO feature from the General track, and this is what it is for.

Contest conventions

From which of the following bands is amateur radio contesting generally excluded?

30 meters.

Thirty metres is unusually constrained, and it is worth separating what is rule from what is convention, because the pool’s answer is the convention:

  • Rule (§97.303(j)): amateur stations on 30 m are secondary to other nations’ fixed services and must accept interference from them and stop causing it.
  • Rule (§97.313(c)(1)): 200 W PEP maximum on 10.10–10.15 MHz — the only HF band with a general power cap below 1500 W.
  • Rule (§97.305): the band is CW and data only; no phone.
  • Convention (ARRL band plan): no contest operating. This is not in Part 97. It is a voluntary band-plan restriction, universally observed, and it exists because 30 m is only 50 kHz wide and shared with services that were there first.

The pool asks about the convention, so “30 meters” is the answer. But if someone contests on 30 m they are being rude, not illegal — while running 500 W there is illegal.

During a VHF/UHF contest, in which band segment would you expect to find the highest level of SSB or CW activity?

In the weak signal segment of the band, with most of the activity near the calling frequency

VHF contesting is weak-signal work: SSB and CW near the bottom of the band, clustered around the calling frequency. Not the FM repeater segment.

What indicator is required to be used by US-licensed operators when operating a station via remote control and the remote transmitter is located in the US?

No additional indicator is required

The transmitter is where it always was, in the US, so nothing changes. Consistent with the General rule that the rules of the transmitter’s location apply.

File formats

Three formats, three purposes, and the pool distinguishes them.

Which of the following file formats is used for exchanging amateur radio log data?

ADIF.

What is the Cabrillo format?

A standard for submission of electronic contest logs.

ADIF moves logs between programs; Cabrillo submits them to a contest sponsor. Different jobs, and sending the wrong one to a contest robot gets your entry rejected.

Confirmations

Which of the following contacts may be confirmed through the Logbook of The World (LoTW)?

All these choices are correct — contacts with amateur stations in any country, contacts made using any mode, and contacts made on any band.

LoTW is universal: any band, any mode, anywhere. Both stations upload cryptographically-signed logs and matching contacts confirm automatically, which replaces the paper QSL card for award purposes.

What is the function of a DX QSL Manager?

Handle the receiving and sending of confirmations for a DX station.

A DXpedition or a station in a country with unreliable post cannot handle thousands of cards. A QSL manager elsewhere does it on their behalf, which is why DX call signs are often published with “QSL via” another call sign.

Remote operation

What indicates the delay between a control operator action and the corresponding change in the transmitted signal?

Latency.

Latency is the practical limit on remote operating. A few tens of milliseconds is unnoticeable; a few hundred makes break-in CW impossible and turns a pile-up into guesswork, because you cannot tell whether the DX came back to you until after you have decided whether to call again.

Mesh networking, again

Which of the following frequencies can be used for amateur radio mesh networks?

Frequencies shared with various unlicensed wireless data services

What type of equipment is commonly used to implement an amateur radio mesh network?

A wireless router running custom firmware.

The Technician answer at Extra level: consumer hardware, amateur firmware, amateur frequencies that happen to overlap the unlicensed bands. And the General rule still holds — you may not communicate with the unlicensed stations sharing them.

Check yourself

  1. A DX station is working split. Where do you transmit, and what do you send?
  2. You want to submit your contest log to the sponsor and also import it into your logging program. Which formats?
  3. Why does 200 ms of latency make remote CW operating difficult?
Answers
  1. On their listening frequency, not their transmit frequency, and send your full call sign once or twice.
  2. Cabrillo to the sponsor, ADIF to the logging program.
  3. Break-in CW depends on hearing the other station between your own characters. With 200 ms of delay you cannot react in time, and in a pile-up you cannot tell whether a reply was meant for you before you must decide to call again.
