Amateur Television, Fast and Slow

Describe NTSC scanning and vestigial sideband, decode an SSTV signal by ear, and say what a DVB-T coding rate means.

10 min readE2BSignals, Modulation & ModesadvancedDraft

Fast-scan analogue television

How many horizontal lines make up a fast-scan (NTSC) television frame?

525.

How is an interlaced scanning pattern generated in a fast-scan (NTSC) television system?

By scanning odd-numbered lines in one field and even-numbered lines in the next

Two fields of 262.5 lines each interleave to make one 525-line frame. Interlacing halves the bandwidth needed for a given apparent refresh rate — the picture updates 60 times a second while only 30 complete frames are sent. A 1930s compression scheme, and an effective one.

What is vestigial sideband modulation?

Amplitude modulation in which one complete sideband and a portion of the other are transmitted.

Which of the following describes the use of vestigial sideband in analog fast-scan TV transmissions?

Vestigial sideband reduces the bandwidth while increasing the fidelity of low frequency video components

A compromise between AM and SSB. Full SSB would be narrowest but makes the receiver’s job much harder for a video signal with content down to DC. Vestigial sideband keeps one full sideband plus a stub of the other, so a simple envelope detector still works while most of the redundant sideband is saved.

What technique allows commercial analog TV receivers to be used for fast-scan TV operations on the 70-centimeter band?

Transmitting on channels shared with cable TV

Cable channel frequencies overlap the 70 cm band, so a cable-ready receiver tunes them directly. That is why amateur fast-scan TV settled on the frequencies it did.

Slow-scan television

SSTV sends a still image through a 3 kHz SSB channel, over seconds to minutes. Every aspect of the signal is a tone.

What kind of receiver can be used to receive and decode SSTV using the Digital Radio Mondiale (DRM) protocol?

SSB

An ordinary SSB receiver plus software. No special hardware at all.

What aspect of an analog slow-scan television signal encodes the brightness of the picture?

Tone frequency.

Brightness is frequency: dark is around 1500 Hz, white around 2300 Hz. Which is why an SSTV signal sounds like a rising and falling warble — you are hearing the picture.

What signals SSTV receiving software to begin a new picture line?

Specific tone frequencies.

A sync tone at 1200 Hz, below the brightness range, marks each line start.

How is color information sent in analog SSTV?

Color lines are sent sequentially.

Red, green, and blue as separate lines in sequence. It is why a mistimed SSTV image shows colour fringing — the components have slipped relative to each other.

What is the function of the vertical interval signaling (VIS) code sent as part of an SSTV transmission?

To identify the SSTV mode being used.

There are many SSTV modes with different resolutions and timings. The VIS code at the start tells the receiving software which one is coming, so it configures itself automatically. Without it you would have to know in advance.

Digital television

Which types of modulation are used for amateur television DVB-T signals?

QAM and QPSK.

The digital modulations from the signals module, carried on OFDM subcarriers — which is what the “T” in DVB-T (terrestrial) implies.

In digital television, what does a coding rate of 3/4 mean?

25% of the data sent is forward error correction data.

A coding rate of 3/4 means three quarters of the transmitted bits are payload and one quarter is forward error correction. Lower coding rates send more redundancy — robust on a poor path, slower on a good one.

That trade is chosen deliberately: a marginal path gets a low coding rate and survives; a strong one gets a high rate and moves more picture.

Check yourself

  1. Why does NTSC interlace rather than scan every line in order?
  2. You hear a warbling tone that rises and falls, with a regular click. What mode, and what are you hearing?
  3. A DVB-T link uses a 1/2 coding rate. What does that tell you about the path?
Answers
  1. Interlacing halves the bandwidth for a given apparent refresh rate: two fields of alternating lines make one frame, updating 60 times a second while sending 30 frames.
  2. SSTV. The warble is brightness encoded as tone frequency; the regular click is the 1200 Hz line sync.
  3. Half the transmitted data is forward error correction, so it is configured for a poor or marginal path — robustness bought with throughput.
