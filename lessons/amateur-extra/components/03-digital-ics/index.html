Digital Integrated Circuits

Compare the logic families, say what hysteresis and tri-state outputs are for, and identify the gate symbols on figure E6-3.

9 min readE6CComponents & CircuitsadvancedDraft

Logic families

Which of the following digital logic families has the lowest power consumption?

CMOS.

Why do CMOS digital integrated circuits have high immunity to noise on the input signal or power supply?

The input switching threshold is about half the power supply voltage.

Both CMOS advantages come from the same design. The threshold sitting at half the supply gives roughly half the supply as noise margin in both directions — far more than TTL’s fixed thresholds, which sit low and give an asymmetric margin.

Which of the following is an advantage of BiCMOS logic?

It has the high input impedance of CMOS and the low output impedance of bipolar transistors.

BiCMOS uses CMOS for the input stages and bipolar for the outputs, taking the better half of each: high impedance where you want to not load the driver, low impedance where you want to drive a load hard.

Tri-state and pull-ups

What is tri-state logic?

Logic devices with 0, 1, and high-impedance output states.

The third state is disconnected — high impedance, neither driving high nor low. It is what lets many devices share one bus: all but one hold their outputs in the high-impedance state, so exactly one drives at a time.

What best describes a pull-up or pull-down resistor?

A resistor connected to the positive or negative supply used to establish a voltage when an input or output is an open circuit

An undriven CMOS input floats and picks up noise, switching randomly and drawing current. A pull-up or pull-down gives it a defined level. This is also what makes a tri-state bus behave when nothing is driving it.

Comparators and hysteresis

What happens when the level of a comparator’s input signal crosses the threshold voltage?

The comparator changes its output state

A comparator is an op-amp used without feedback: its output slams to one rail or the other depending on which input is higher. It turns an analogue level into a logic level.

What is the function of hysteresis in a comparator?

To prevent input noise from causing unstable output signals.

Without hysteresis, an input sitting near the threshold with a little noise on it makes the output chatter — many transitions where one was intended.

Hysteresis moves the threshold depending on the current output state: once the output has gone high, the input must fall appreciably below the original threshold to bring it back. That gap swallows the noise, and one clean transition results.

This is what a Schmitt trigger is, and it is why squaring up a noisy signal needs one rather than a plain comparator.

Programmable logic

What is used to design the configuration of a field-programmable gate array (FPGA)?

Hardware description language (HDL)

An FPGA is a fabric of configurable logic. You describe the behaviour you want in an HDL — Verilog or VHDL — and a tool synthesises a configuration.

This is how modern SDRs are built: the DSP chain from the circuits module is HDL running in an FPGA, which is why the same hardware can gain new modes through a firmware update.

Figure E6-3

Three symbols the pool asks for:

  • Symbol 2: NAND gate
  • Symbol 4: NOR gate
  • Symbol 5: NOT (inverter)

Reading gate symbols is two observations:

  • Shape gives the base function. A flat-backed D is AND; a curved-back shield is OR; a triangle is a buffer or inverter.
  • A small circle (bubble) on the output inverts it. AND plus bubble is NAND; OR plus bubble is NOR; buffer plus bubble is NOT.

Two rules, six symbols.

Check yourself

  1. Several devices share one data line. What output type do they need, and what holds the line when none is driving?
  2. Your comparator chatters on a slowly-changing input. What does it need?
  3. Which shape and marking make a NOR gate?
Answers
  1. Tri-state outputs, so all but one sit at high impedance — plus a pull-up or pull-down resistor to define the line when nothing drives it.
  2. Hysteresis — a Schmitt trigger. It separates the two thresholds so noise cannot cause repeated transitions.
  3. A curved-back OR shape with a bubble on the output.
