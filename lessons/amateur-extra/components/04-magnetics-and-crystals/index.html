Cores, Inductors, and Piezoelectricity

Choose a core material by what it must do, explain eddy-current loss and saturation, and describe a crystal's equivalent circuit.

10 min readE6DComponents & CircuitsadvancedDraft

What a core does

What core material property determines the inductance of an inductor?

Permeability.

Permeability is how readily a material carries magnetic flux. A high-permeability core concentrates the field, so the same winding has far more inductance.

How do ferrite and powdered iron compare for use in an inductor core?

Ferrite cores generally require fewer turns to produce a given inductance value.

Ferrite has much higher permeability, so fewer turns. Powdered iron is lower permeability and:

Which of the following materials has the highest temperature stability of its magnetic characteristics?

Powdered iron

So the choice is a genuine trade: ferrite for compactness, powdered iron for stability. A tuned circuit that must not drift uses powdered iron; a broadband transformer or common-mode choke uses ferrite.

And the counterintuitive third option:

Which type of core material decreases inductance when inserted into a coil?

Brass.

Brass is not magnetic, so it does not concentrate flux — but it conducts, so the coil’s field induces eddy currents in it that oppose the field. The net effect reduces inductance. That is how a brass tuning slug works, and why turning a slug inward can move frequency in either direction depending on what the slug is made of.

Losses

Why are cores of inductors and transformers sometimes constructed of thin layers?

To reduce power loss from eddy currents in the core

A solid conductive core has currents induced in it, and those currents heat it. Slicing the core into insulated laminations breaks the paths those currents would take. It is why mains transformer cores are stacks of thin sheets, and why RF cores are ferrite or powdered iron — non-conductive materials, where the problem does not arise.

What causes inductor saturation?

Operation at excessive magnetic flux.

Push enough current through and the core’s magnetic domains are all aligned; it cannot carry more flux. Inductance collapses toward the air-core value, the current rises sharply, and in a switching supply that is how the transistors die. Saturation is a current limit, not a voltage one, and it is why inductors carry a saturation-current rating separate from their heating rating.

What is a primary advantage of using a toroidal core instead of a solenoidal core in an inductor?

Toroidal cores confine most of the magnetic field within the core material.

Self-shielding, as the General track noted — no coupling to neighbours in either direction.

What devices are commonly used as VHF and UHF parasitic suppressors at the input and output terminals of a transistor HF amplifier?

Ferrite beads

A bead adds loss specifically at VHF while leaving HF nearly untouched, which is exactly what kills the parasitic oscillation the amplifiers lesson described without affecting the wanted signal.

Piezoelectricity

What is piezoelectricity?

A characteristic of materials that generate a voltage when stressed and that flex when a voltage is applied

Which of the following is an aspect of the piezoelectric effect?

Mechanical deformation of material due to the application of a voltage.

Both directions. Squeeze it and get a voltage; apply a voltage and it deforms. A quartz crystal in an oscillator does both continuously, which is why it resonates mechanically at a frequency set by its physical dimensions — and why it is so much more stable than an LC circuit.

What is the equivalent circuit of a quartz crystal?

Series RLC in parallel with a shunt C representing electrode and stray capacitance.

Worth understanding rather than memorising, because it explains the crystal’s two resonances. The series RLC is the mechanical resonance — very high Q, very low R. The shunt C is the electrical capacitance of the electrodes and holder.

Together they give a series resonance (where the RLC branch is at minimum impedance) and, a little higher, a parallel resonance (where the RLC branch’s net inductance resonates with the shunt C). Those two are only a few kilohertz apart, and which one an oscillator uses is why the load capacitance specification from the oscillators lesson matters.

Check yourself

  1. You need an inductor whose value must not drift with temperature. Ferrite or powdered iron?
  2. Your switching supply’s inductor gets hot and the current waveform spikes sharply. What is happening?
  3. Why does a brass slug reduce inductance when an iron one increases it?
Answers
  1. Powdered iron — highest temperature stability of permeability. Ferrite would need fewer turns but drift more.
  2. Saturation. The core cannot carry more flux, inductance collapses, and current rises steeply.
  3. Brass is non-magnetic but conductive: the coil’s field induces opposing eddy currents in it, reducing inductance. Iron concentrates flux and increases it.
