Transmission Lines as Circuit Elements

Predict what a shorted or open stub looks like at any of four lengths, compute physical length from velocity factor, and compare line types honestly.

Velocity factor

What is the velocity factor of a transmission line?

The velocity of a wave in the transmission line divided by the velocity of light in a vacuum.

Which of the following has the biggest effect on the velocity factor of a transmission line?

The insulating dielectric material.

The dielectric, not the conductor. Solid polyethylene gives about 0.66; foam about 0.80; air-spaced parallel line about 0.95.

Why is the electrical length of a coaxial cable longer than its physical length?

Electromagnetic waves move more slowly in a coaxial cable than in air.

Slower propagation means more cycles fit in a given physical distance, so the cable is electrically longer than it looks.

Worked, as the pool asks it: the physical length of an air-insulated parallel conductor line that is a half wavelength at 14.10 MHz.

Free-space half wavelength = 150 / 14.10 = 10.64 m. With a velocity factor of about 0.95 — nearly one, since it is air-insulated — the answer is close to 10.6 metres. That the answer is so nearly the free-space figure is the point of an air-insulated line.

Stubs: the four lengths

A length of line with its far end shorted or open behaves, at the near end, as a reactance — and the behaviour cycles every half wavelength. Four cases, all asked:

LengthShorted far endOpen far end
1/8 wavelengthinductive reactancecapacitive reactance
1/4 wavelengthvery high impedancevery low impedance
3/8 wavelengthcapacitiveinductive
1/2 wavelengthvery low impedancevery high impedance

Rather than memorise eight cells, hold two rules:

A quarter-wave line inverts. Short becomes open, open becomes short. That is also why the quarter-wave transformer works.

A half-wave line repeats. Whatever is at the far end appears at the near end. A shorted half wave looks like a short; an open half wave looks like an open.

The eighth-wave cases are halfway to the inversion, so they are pure reactances rather than extremes — and their sign follows from which extreme they are heading toward.

The practical payoff: a shorted quarter-wave stub is a very high impedance at its design frequency and a short circuit at twice it. Hang one on a transmitter’s output and it passes the fundamental while shorting the second harmonic to ground. That is a harmonic filter made of a piece of coax.

Line types compared

How does parallel conductor transmission line compare to coaxial cable with a plastic dielectric?

Lower loss

Parallel line has more conductor surface, more air dielectric, and no lossy jacket in the field. It is genuinely better — and it must be kept clear of metal and of people, cannot be buried or run through a wall, and radiates if unbalanced. The trade is real in both directions.

Which of the following is a significant difference between foam dielectric coaxial cable and solid dielectric coaxial cable, assuming all other parameters are the same?

All these choices are correct — foam has lower loss per unit length, higher velocity factor, and absorbs more moisture.

The last one is the catch. Foam is better electrically and worse at keeping water out once the jacket is breached — which, given that moisture contamination is the leading cause of coax failure, is not a small consideration.

What is microstrip?

Precision printed circuit conductors above a ground plane that provide constant impedance interconnects at microwave frequencies.

At microwave, a track on a circuit board is a transmission line whether you designed it as one or not. Microstrip is the discipline of designing it deliberately — controlled width, controlled substrate, controlled impedance.

Check yourself

  1. A shorted quarter-wave stub at 14 MHz. What does it look like at 14 MHz, and at 28 MHz?
  2. You need a half-wavelength phasing line at 21 MHz using coax with a 0.66 velocity factor. Physical length?
  3. Why is foam coax not automatically the better choice?
Answers
  1. Very high impedance at 14 MHz (a quarter wave inverts the short), and a short circuit at 28 MHz (where it is a half wave, which repeats the short).
  2. Free-space half wave = 150 / 21 = 7.14 m; × 0.66 = 4.71 metres.
  3. It absorbs more moisture once the jacket is breached, and moisture contamination is the leading cause of coax failure — against lower loss and a higher velocity factor.
