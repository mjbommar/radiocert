Who May Hold a GMRS Licence, and Who May Use It

State who is eligible, who may operate under someone else's licence, how long a licence runs, and what the application actually asks for.

8 min readRules & RegulationsintroDraft

The unusual thing about GMRS

Almost every licence the FCC issues authorises one person. GMRS authorises a household.

That single fact is why GMRS is worth the paperwork even if you already hold an amateur licence: your family can legally use the radios, on real power, without any of them passing an exam. An amateur licence never permits that.

Eligibility

Only an individual who is at least 18 years old and who meets the requirements of § 95.305 is eligible to obtain a new individual GMRS licence. — 47 CFR 95.1705(a)(1)

Three consequences worth reading carefully:

  • An individual, not an organisation. New GMRS licences go to people. (Some corporate licences granted long ago persist, but none are issued now.)
  • 18 or older to hold one. There is no minimum age to operate.
  • No exam, no demonstration of knowledge. You apply, you pay, you are licensed. The rules still bind you exactly as if there had been an exam, which is the whole reason this track exists.

Who may operate under it

The licensee may operate their own stations, and:

Any individual who holds an individual license may allow his or her immediate family members to operate his or her GMRS stations. — 47 CFR 95.1705(c)(2)

“Immediate family” is the category the rule uses, and it is meant plainly: spouse, children, parents, grandparents, grandchildren, siblings, and their in-laws. Friends and neighbours are not covered — they need their own licence.

You remain responsible. Section 95.1705(b) makes the licence holder responsible at all times for the proper operation of the stations. And §95.1743 is explicit that an operator under 18 is not personally responsible for improper operation of a repeater or base station; the licensee is solely responsible.

Handing a radio to your ten-year-old is legal. It is also you who answers for what they transmit.

Term and renewal

Each individual license in the GMRS will normally have a term of ten years from the date of grant or renewal. — 47 CFR 95.1705(e)

Ten years, renewable through the ordinary Part 1 procedures. The rule notes the FCC may grant a shorter term at renewal as a sanction — a reminder that the licence is a privilege with conditions rather than a receipt for a payment.

What the application involves

The process runs through the FCC’s Universal Licensing System:

  1. Get an FRN — an FCC Registration Number, obtained from the CORES system. One per person, reused for every FCC dealing you ever have.
  2. File the GMRS application in ULS under your FRN.
  3. Pay the application fee. Fees change by Report and Order; check the current amount at filing rather than trusting any figure written down here.
  4. Wait for the grant. Your call sign appears in the ULS database, and that database entry is the licence — there is no certificate to wait for.

Your call sign will look like WRXX123: a four-letter prefix beginning with W, then three digits. Unlike amateur call signs, you do not choose it and there is no vanity system.

Before the grant arrives

Do not transmit. You may listen all you like — receiving is unregulated — and listening is genuinely the best preparation. Turn the tone squelch off, scan the 462 MHz channels, and find out what is actually active where you live before you decide which channel matters and whether a repeater is in range.

Check yourself

  1. Your brother-in-law wants to carry one of your GMRS radios on a group hike. Is that permitted?
  2. Your fourteen-year-old transmits something they should not have. Who is responsible under the rules?
  3. How long does a GMRS licence run, and where does the rule say so?
Answers
  1. Yes — immediate family includes in-laws, and family members may operate the licensee’s stations under §95.1705(c)(2). A friend on the same hike could not.
  2. You are. Section 95.1705(b) makes the licensee responsible at all times, and §95.1743 removes personal responsibility from operators under 18.
  3. Ten years, per §95.1705(e), renewable under Part 1.
