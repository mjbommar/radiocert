Sharing Frequencies, Nets, and RACES

Check whether a frequency is in use, allow the right spacing for your mode, handle a distress call, and apply the RACES drill limits.

10 min readG2BOperating ProceduresintroDraft

Nobody owns a frequency

Which of the following is true concerning access to frequencies?

Except during emergencies, no amateur station has priority access to any frequency.

Not the net that has met there for thirty years, not the station with the biggest antenna, not whoever was there first. The only exception is an emergency.

Which is why the corresponding rule when propagation changes is negotiation rather than assertion:

What is good amateur practice if propagation changes during a contact creating interference from other stations using the frequency?

Attempt to resolve the interference problem with the other stations in a mutually acceptable manner

This case is genuinely nobody’s fault. Two stations that could not hear each other when they started now can, because the ionosphere changed. Both were operating correctly.

Checking before you transmit

How can you avoid harmful interference on an apparently clear frequency before calling CQ on CW or phone?

Send “QRL?” on CW, followed by your call sign; or, if using phone, ask if the frequency is in use, followed by your call sign

“Apparently clear” is doing the work in that question. You may be inside someone’s skip zone, or hearing only one side of a contact. Asking costs three seconds and prevents transmitting on top of a conversation you cannot hear.

QRL? means “are you busy / is this frequency in use?” — and you append your call sign, because an unidentified question is an unidentified transmission.

Spacing

Different modes need different room, and the pool gives both numbers.

When selecting a CW transmitting frequency, what minimum separation from other stations should be used to minimize interference to stations on adjacent frequencies?

150 Hz to 500 Hz

When selecting an SSB transmitting frequency, what minimum separation should be used to minimize interference to stations on adjacent frequencies?

2 kHz to 3 kHz

Both follow from bandwidth: CW occupies about 150 Hz, SSB about 3 kHz, so the spacing is roughly one signal width. Sitting closer than your own bandwidth guarantees you are interfering with someone.

Which of the following complies with commonly accepted amateur practice when choosing a frequency on which to initiate a call?

Follow the voluntary band plan.

A distress call

What is the first thing you should do if you are communicating with another amateur station and hear a station in distress break in?

Acknowledge the station in distress and determine what assistance may be needed.

Acknowledge and find out what is needed. Not “stand by”, not finish your conversation, not call someone else first. Acknowledge, then establish what the situation is — because everything after that depends on knowing it.

Net management

Which of the following is good amateur practice for net management?

Have a backup frequency in case of interference or poor conditions.

Announced in advance, so that when the primary becomes unusable the net reforms instead of dissolving. Simple, and routinely forgotten until it is needed.

RACES, more precisely

The Technician track introduced RACES; General adds two constraints.

Who may be the control operator of an amateur station transmitting in RACES to assist relief operations during a disaster?

Only a person holding an FCC-issued amateur operator license.

An amateur licence is necessary — and, as the Technician material covered, civil-defence enrolment certification is necessary too. Both, not either.

How often may RACES training drills and tests be routinely conducted without special authorization?

No more than 1 hour per week.

One hour a week. More requires authorisation, because RACES operates under emergency authority and routine use of that authority would erode it.

Check yourself

  1. You want to call CQ on what sounds like an empty frequency. What do you send first?
  2. You are in a conversation when someone breaks in reporting an accident. What is your first action?
  3. Your RACES group wants two hours of drill a week. Permitted?
Answers
  1. “QRL?” followed by your call sign on CW, or ask whether the frequency is in use followed by your call sign on phone.
  2. Acknowledge the station in distress and determine what assistance is needed.
  3. Not routinely. RACES drills are limited to one hour per week without special authorisation.
