About

This is self-study courseware for the FCC and FAA radio credentials, built as a knowledge graph rather than a book. Lessons, flashcards, figures, and the practice engine all derive from one set of authored files plus primary sources fetched by hash — so a claim can be traced to the document it came from, and a stale one shows up as a build failure rather than as a wrong answer on exam day.

It is free, it stores nothing about you anywhere but your own browser, and it is nobody's product. The formal terms are on the licences page; the provenance of every source document is on the sources page.

What made it possible

Three bodies of work this is built on top of, and could not substitute for.

  • NCVEC Question Pool Committeevolunteer examiners

    Writes and maintains every amateur examination question, then releases each pool into the public domain. Not merely published because the rules require it — given away. This project would not exist otherwise.

  • Office of the Federal RegistereCFR

    Serves the entire Code of Federal Regulations as structured XML, free and versioned by date. Every regulatory claim here is pinned to a dated fetch because of it.

  • FCC, FAA, and NISTUS Government

    Public notices, Part 107 and Part 89, and the WWV time-service standard — all published openly and, as works of the US Government, free of copyright.

A note on the pools

The Question Pool Committee deserves the top of this page rather than a footnote. Writing and maintaining roughly fourteen hundred examination questions across three licence classes, revising them on a four-year cycle, and issuing errata when the rules change is substantial, unglamorous, volunteer work. Releasing the result into the public domain — not merely publishing it because 47 CFR 97.523 says they must — is what lets anyone build something like this without asking permission.

NCVEC is not affiliated with this project and does not endorse it. Nothing here is an official publication of the FCC, the FAA, the NCVEC, or the ARRL, and where this material and the regulation disagree, the regulation governs.

Study material derived from primary sources: NCVEC question pools published under 47 CFR 97.523, and regulation text from eCFR. Nothing here is legal advice or an official FCC or FAA publication.
